# Routing economics audit — 2026-09-26

[Русская версия](AUDIT.ru.md) · [Raw benchmarks](https://github.com/suenot/codex-jev-router-benchmarks/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md)

## Finding

The installed router chooses the **model of a subagent after Codex has decided to spawn one**. It does not choose whether to delegate, and it cannot change the model of the active parent turn. This is the central reason its cheap child models did not save money on short, serial work. The [router](src/router.mjs) accepts a role and task summary and always returns a model and effort; the [installer](scripts/install.mjs) tells the parent to call it before each spawn. The only logged outcome is the chosen route, not total task cost or success ([decision log](src/log-decision.mjs)).

The measured prices below are **estimates using published Standard, short-context API rates**, not Codex subscription charges. They count Sol parent, child, and Jev where present. The benchmark called Jev before starting the parent, so any parent tokens for calling the routing command itself are omitted. See the [method and raw data](https://github.com/suenot/codex-jev-router-benchmarks/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md#method-and-limits-of-the-new-comparison).

| Experiment | Direct Sol high | Routed Sol-high parent | Routed children | Jev | Routed total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 runs of 12 read-only tasks | $0.452677 | $0.472191 | $0.184278 | $0.000632 | $0.657100 |
| Six four-task groups using the same tasks | $0.193704 | $0.342128 | $0.204488 | $0.000632 | $0.547248 |
| One Django code fix; official local test passes in both arms | $0.144088 | $0.047012 | $0.009622 | $0.000026 | $0.056660 |

We also tested the proposed boundary directly: Jev selected the **root session's** model and effort before each of the same 24 read-only task runs. There was no Sol parent or subagent. This arm cost an estimated **$0.183746** in Codex calls plus **$0.000632** for Jev, **$0.184377 total versus $0.452677** for the previously saved direct Sol-high control (**59.3% less**). Strict passes were **21/24 versus 22/24**, and summed time including Jev was **775.6 versus 735.8 seconds** (**5.4% longer**). The eight Luna-low lookups cost $0.005828 versus $0.112199; eight Luna-medium extractions cost $0.010099 versus $0.215430. Eight **Sol-low judgments cost $0.168450 versus $0.125048**, a **34.7% increase**. This was a later comparison against historical Sol-high runs, not a randomized A/B test or a claim of equal quality. [Runner, grades, raw usage, and traces](https://github.com/suenot/codex-jev-router-benchmarks/blob/main/BENCHMARK.md#direct-routing-before-the-root-codex-turn).

For the 24 short-task runs, the Sol parent alone cost **$0.019514 more** than direct Sol before its children started. Only **3 of 24** routed pairs were cheaper. With the measured child cost, the parent would have to fall from $0.472191 to at most **$0.267767**, a **43.3% reduction**, just to break even. In the four-task groups, children plus Jev already cost **$0.205120**, more than the **$0.193704** direct baseline; a free parent could not make those recorded runs cheaper. By contrast, the one code fix had enough direct Sol work to cover the $0.047012 parent and $0.009622 Luna child. Its routed arm cost **60.7% less** but took **50.5% longer**. One paired fix cannot establish a general saving.

### What caused the overhead

1. **An unnecessary second Codex session.** The per-task harness explicitly forced one child for every task, including an exact symbol lookup. The parent still consumed a full Sol context and model turns to spawn, wait, and return the answer. The four-task harness forced four fresh children instead of letting one Sol session reuse its context. These are valid measurements of *forced delegation*, not evidence that Jev would choose delegation for every real task.
2. **Some routes cannot save much per token.** Focused judgments were routed to **Sol low**; Sol low and Sol high share the same per-token model rates, so only fewer tokens or turns could offset another session. In eight runs, direct Sol cost $0.125048 while routed Sol parents cost $0.159548 and children another $0.162436. Luna-low children for exact lookups cost only $0.010408 across eight runs, but their parents cost $0.151556 against a $0.112199 direct baseline. Bounded extraction with Luna medium was the one source-task category with a lower aggregate routed cost ($0.172733 versus $0.215430), though only three of its eight individual pairs saved money. [Sol and Luna prices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing).
3. **The decision model is not the expensive part.** Jev added about $0.000632 to each 24-task arrangement, below 0.1% of the routed source-task cost. Replacing it with Laya, Kev, or another local decider might improve privacy, latency, or decision quality, but removing its entire fee would not reverse either losing comparison.
4. **Caching did not erase the additional work.** About 90.5% of parent input in the separate-task runs and 92.8% in the batch runs was reported cached, yet the parent charges remained $0.472191 and $0.342128. A different prefix or cache policy might help, but the current data do not identify cache misses as the primary loss. [OpenAI's cache guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching) explains that reuse needs a matching rendered prefix and can change when the model or tools change.

## Why other routers can look better

These projects optimize **different boundaries**; their published results are not interchangeable:

| Boundary | Examples | What the published evidence means here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typed decision backend | [JevRouter](https://github.com/BillionsBobby/JevRouter#benchmark), [Laya](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya), [Kev](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev), [SemIf](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf-OpenJev), [NanoJev](https://github.com/TianyuCodings/NanoJev), [AnyJev](https://github.com/nokia-applied-research/AnyJev) | They can make a choice cheaply or locally. JevRouter's cited benchmark predicts the first five tool calls on ten tasks and explicitly does **not** measure completed-task cost. Swapping this layer leaves our Sol parent and child topology intact. Local probabilities also need calibration before reusing Jev thresholds. |
| Pre-turn harness routing | [jev-harness-router](https://github.com/JoacoMarc/jev-harness-router#running-it-on-the-claude-agent-sdk) | It sends the chosen model and effort to the next Claude Agent SDK query **before** the agent turn. Its `savings` command prices a model mix assuming equal token counts; the author explicitly warns that extra turns can erase the estimate. The project also reports poor tier decisions on short, context-dependent messages until recent context is supplied. |
| Codex request proxy | [flaviusapop/jev-router](https://github.com/flaviusapop/jev-router#how-it-works) | It runs the real Codex CLI through a loopback proxy and rewrites the model and effort for each new turn, without creating a Sol parent just for routing. It pins continuations and separately keys child conversations; its cache guard delays downgrades in long sessions. Its documented default Codex tiers use GPT-5.6 models, so this user's GPT-6 Sol/Luna policy would require configuration and evaluation. The proxy handles authenticated requests and relies on request internals; its README describes Codex 0.60.0 testing, while this benchmark used 0.157.1. We have not reproduced its savings. |
| Request proxy or step routing | [RouteLLM](https://github.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM), [FrugalGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176), [AgentRouter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22951) | These choose the serving model for a request or agent step, without adding a Sol parent just to choose a child. Reported savings come from their own workloads and quality measures. AgentRouter reports trajectory-level quality; RouteLLM and FrugalGPT mainly benchmark individual requests. None proves savings for this Codex installation. |
| Codex/Claude request interception and context editing | [jev-routing](https://github.com/nekowasabi/jev-routing#context-editing-what-we-tried) | Its own Codex/Claude experiments include increases as well as decreases. It estimates an upper bound of roughly 6.3% token reduction from one context-clearing approach across its real sessions and says the price-weighted version is roughly cost-neutral. This is the author's estimate, not a replication on our tasks. |

Official [OpenAI multi-agent guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/responses-multi-agent#when-to-use-multi-agent) says subagents help when work divides into independent bounded parts and explicitly warns they can increase tokens on short or sequential work. [Observability guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents-api/observability) requires counting root and child work, retries, cache usage, and applicable tools. This is the accounting gap in the earlier 71% and 98.3% worker-only comparisons.

## Repair path

1. **Gate delegation before Jev.** For an exact lookup, short serial source or log search, or a focused claim that the active parent can check directly, use tools in the parent and skip the Jev call. Spawn when parallelism, isolated context, or an independent review has a concrete benefit. This avoids the measured extra session cost; it does **not** by itself make the direct Sol task cheaper.
2. **For actual cost savings, route the working turn before it starts.** The direct-root pilot above supports this architecture for the selected source tasks, while its one fewer strict pass prevents an equal-quality claim. A separate Codex launcher or App Server client can call the decider first and pass the chosen `model` and `effort` to [`turn/start`](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/app-server#start-a-turn). A Codex request proxy is another demonstrated integration pattern, but its request rewrite and credential flow require separate compatibility and security review. This keeps one agent instead of a Sol parent plus child. The existing AGENTS.md instruction cannot retroactively change the active parent's model. A `UserPromptSubmit` hook can add context or block a prompt, but its [documented output](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/hooks#userpromptsubmit) does not include a model override. Model switches in a continuing thread need their own cache and quality measurements.
3. **Keep child routing for work that already merits a child.** Its incremental comparison is `Sol parent + fixed Sol-high child` versus `Sol parent + routed child`, not against one direct Sol session. Do not claim that Sol low is cheaper per token than Sol high. The Sol-high fallback and no-Astra policy remain sensible quality controls; child thresholds should be fitted to observed end-to-end success and cost, not decider confidence alone.
4. **Make cost and quality observable.** Save root and child usage, selected model, cache and reasoning tokens, route cost, elapsed time, retries, final tests, and failure corrections for the same task ID. The current route log stores only model and reason. Add a compact prior-task state for context-dependent descriptions, rather than trusting an isolated short summary. The direct-root pilot reused a policy written for **child** tasks; fit root routing and local-decider scores on labeled root-turn outcomes before enabling an automatic downgrade.

## Evidence still needed

The 12 source tasks were deliberately chosen as candidates for cheap routes, repeated twice, and both batch and direct-root arrangements reuse them. The direct-root experiment was run after the saved Sol-high control, without randomized arm order. Strict JSON/citation failures sometimes contained the correct factual answer but still failed the requested contract. The code fix is one pair graded with a local official test, not the full SWE-bench Docker harness. The fresh isolated benchmark homes omit the user's normal plugins, rules, and skill hook. The user's current parent configuration is Sol **xhigh**, while the benchmark used Sol **high** by agreement. The price figures therefore do not predict the actual subscription usage or the exact live installation.

The next benchmark should run four arms together on a mixed workload: **one Sol high**, **one directly routed root**, **Sol parent + fixed Sol-high child**, and **Sol parent + routed child**. Include continuing threads, serial and parallel tasks, edits with hidden tests, web and log searches, retries, and quality grading of the completed task. Randomize arm order and use enough repetitions to estimate variance. Compare a simple deterministic gate with Jev, and require a quality floor before optimizing dollars or elapsed time. [OpenAI's agent evaluation guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agent-evals) supports judging complete traces rather than only a route label.
